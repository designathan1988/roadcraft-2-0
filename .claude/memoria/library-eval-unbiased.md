When the owner sends libraries, tools or assets, the bar is the game itself. On 2026-10-25 he said: "TEM QUE SER MELHOR QUE O JOGO NA QUESTÃO DE TEMPO, POLIGONOS, DESEMPENHO, QUALIDADE GRAFICA, FUNCIONALIDES, ETC.. SE FOR PIOR NÃO QUEREMOS".

Earlier the same day he called the first round a fraud: more than 10 libraries, and every one was rejected ("POSSO TRAZER MAIS 100 BIBLIOTECAS QUE TODAS SERÃO REPROVADAS"). The fault was in the fairness, not in comparing with the game:
- library defaults were set against the game's tuned pipeline;
- our own setup bugs were blamed on the libraries;
- libraries were used for the wrong job;
- the "no visible change" optimization rule was applied to features;
- my summaries said "don't use" even when the reports found useful parts.

**Why:** the owner wants real improvements, and a biased test hides them.

**How to apply:**
- Set each library up at its best: read its docs and examples, tune it, fix setup problems, use it for what it is good at.
- Measure it against the game on the same case, axis by axis: time, triangles, draw calls and frame cost, graphic quality (images side by side), features.
- Give a scorecard per item: better, same or worse on each axis.
- The owner then sharpened the bar: it must be better on EVERY axis ("É SER MELHOR EM TUDO. SE NÃO FOR MELHOR PARA QUE VAMOS QUERER?"). Adopt only what wins or ties on every axis and wins on at least one. Adopting only a part counts; it is judged on the axes that part touches.
- Say no only on a measured, fairly obtained loss.
- Report what each one adds and its cost; never collapse it to "nothing works".
- Related: [[optimization-no-visible-change]] covers only optimizations.
