When I supervise the working sessions (VIGIA role), every stage is judged on the code AND the images: I open each before/after picture myself and send it to the user (SendUserFile), never accepting a stage on polygon counts or diffs alone. Also judge whether the stage achieves the goal (a real architecture change, not a 2 % patch), not only whether its numbers are honest. No city benchmarks or game runs to measure: the measure is the polygon count plus photos.

**Why:** the user asked "onde estão as imagens? como está sabendo se está certo?" after I accepted a buildings stage without opening its photos, and earlier called me out for approving honest-but-tiny patches and for running a city benchmark they never asked for.

**How to apply:** on every delivery, list the evidence images, Read them, describe what they show, send them to the user, and order the sessions to produce side-by-side PNGs for every changed piece. Related: [[rebuild-dont-patch]].

**Faces (2026-10-25):** I accepted an open mouth after only one defect had gone. The upper teeth were still missing, the upper gum was bare and the brows were pulled down, and the owner caught it: "TÁ CEGO?". Never accept a face on a partial fix. Before accepting a person, open every face channel the game plays, at game scale and at 2x. Check teeth top and bottom, mouth cavity, gums, tongue, brows and eyes, against the source render. One remaining defect means the face is rejected.

**Judge against the owner's words, not the agent's report (2026-10-25).** I passed the UI prototype v1 because the screenshots looked tidy and the HUD % went down. The owner: "VOCÊ SÓ DEIXA PASSAR TUDO". The prototype ignored most of what he asked for: a new look (it kept the same colours), only the tools at the bottom (it moved everything there), panels that don't change size (they did), and research done properly.

**How to apply:** before opening any deliverable, write the owner's requirements for it as a checklist, taken from his own messages. Tick each item against the images and the code. One unmet item means rejected, however good the rest looks or how confident the agent's report sounds.
