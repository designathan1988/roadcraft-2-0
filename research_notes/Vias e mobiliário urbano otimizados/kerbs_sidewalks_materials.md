# Kerbs, sidewalks, verges and road/sidewalk materials: cheap modeling and texturing for a three.js r186 / WebGL2 city builder

Scope note: about 20 search and fetch calls. Primary sources were found for hex-tiling (Mikkelsen JCGT 2022, read in full from the PDF text), Burley's 2019 follow-up, Unity stochastic texturing, the City Sample / Matrix Awakens pipeline, the three.js loader and array-texture docs, the Unreal mesh-decal docs, and the CS1 LOD limits. I found **no** primary public documentation on how CS2, Transport Fever 2, SimCity 2013, GTA or Watch Dogs build kerb geometry, or on their triangle budgets. Those points are listed under Gaps, and any claims about them are marked as inferences.

## 1. How shipped games and samples build kerbs and sidewalks (CS1/CS2, TF2, SimCity 2013, City Sample, open worlds); triangle budgets

### Takeaway
The sources agree on a pattern, though for most titles it is only partly documented: city builders ship a road *segment* mesh that is extruded or deformed along the curve, with a shared texture atlas, and kerbs and sidewalks are part of that cross-section. City Sample builds roads, sidewalks and kerbs procedurally (Houdini → point cloud → instances), uses very little custom geometry, gets its variation from procedural materials over Quixel base textures, and uses mesh decals for road markings. Kerb triangle budgets are tiny. CS1 treats a LOD of 10–100 triangles as typical.

### Cited Findings
- City Sample generation stages run in order: City Layout → Roads → Freeway → Lots and Sidewalks → Traffic/Buildings/Ground → Street Furniture, Decals, Audio. Houdini exports a large point cloud, which a Rule Processor turns into thousands of UE5 instances. — [Epic, City Sample project docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/city-sample-project-unreal-engine-demonstration)
- City Sample: "Very little custom geometry is used in the city". "Custom meshes are used for collisions, the freeway deck, road decals, and custom roofs". Street props, decals and trash use Nanite. — [Epic, City Sample project docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/city-sample-project-unreal-engine-demonstration)
- A Matrix Awakens artist says sidewalks and curbs started from Quixel base textures with custom changes. Their variation comes from procedural texturing in the engine material, which gives a more organic look, and the road textures used UV tricks to help with seams. — [zolord ArtStation, The Matrix Awakens](https://zolord.artstation.com/projects/3qZYPm)
- Another City Sample artist focused on procedural textures and material graphs for freeways, roads, rooftops and sidewalks, and worked with outsourcers so assets fit the procedural assembly pipeline. — [fatrobot ArtStation, Matrix Awakens - UE5](https://fatrobot.artstation.com/projects/Pen5PL)
- Houdini workflow talk for the city: [SideFX, The Matrix Awakens: Generating a World](https://www.sidefx.com/learn/talks/the-matrix-awakens-generating-a-world/). Epic's step-by-step guide: [City Sample Quick Start – Houdini](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/city-sample-quick-start-for-generating-a-city-and-freeway-using-houdini)
- CS1 road segments are .obj meshes mapped into a single 1024×1024 UV map, and the mesh must not run off its edge. Adding a curb to one side of a segment mesh broke the pavement look until the texture maps were re-exported with the same UV layout. — [Paradox forum, road modding help](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/road-modding-help.1190813)
- CS1 Road Editor (Green Cities) is the official tool for custom roads. — [Paradox forum, Green Cities Dev Diary 8: Road Modding](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/cities-skylines-green-cities-dev-diary-8-road-modding.1050864/page-2)
- CS1 LOD budget (Loading Screen Mod author): the warning fires at about 600 triangles or 1000 vertices. The game rejects LODs over 4062 vertices, or about 8100 for buildings and nets, and "10 to 100 triangles is rather typical for a LOD mesh". — [Steam, Loading Screen Mod comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/667342976) (snippet found via search; the exact thread page is [this discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/discussion/667342976/1639789306562193628))
- CS2 dev diary #1 (road tools) covers road placement, pedestrian streets, paths, premade intersections, and helper lines for steepness, angles and crossings. It gives no kerb mesh details. — [Paradox, CS2 Road Tools feature](https://paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/features/road-tools); [Game Developer, how roads became the backbone of CS2](https://gamasutra.com/design/how-colossal-order-turned-roads-into-the-backbone-of-cities-skylines-ii)
- Transport Fever 2 street types are Lua configs in `res/config/street/` (registered via a `streetTypeRep`), with UI icons in `res/textures/ui/streets/`. — [Steam Workshop TF2 discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/2021038808); [GamingOnLinux, TF2 modding expansion](https://gamingonlinux.com/2020/06/transport-fever-2-gets-much-expanded-modding-and-new-features)
- A commercial UE road plugin uses three UV channels: (1) planar for seamless, non-stretching base textures, (2) a continuous linear channel along the road length for lane markings, and (3) a normalized channel for masking. Another tool puts a separate track per lane in UV0 and left/right side tracks in UV1. — [Fab listing, road tool (UV channels)](https://www.fab.com/listings/34605f9d-e5e8-4a71-a64f-5bf230da274c); [Unreal Drive docs, Build Mesh Tool](https://unrealdrive.readthedocs.io/en/v2.0.0/ProcedureGenerationTool.html)
- Unreal Drive uses vertex color to control where stretched textures appear, which removes seam artifacts where several road splines intersect. — [Unreal Drive docs](https://unrealdrive.readthedocs.io/en/v2.0.0/ProcedureGenerationTool.html)
- A procedural spline road tool for s&box also places sidewalks, decals and streetlights along the spline. — [sbox.game roadtool](https://sbox.game/redsnail/roadtool)

### Inferences
- For a huge three.js city, the City Sample split is the right model: (a) a procedural cross-section sweep for road, gutter, kerb and sidewalk, (b) material-level variation instead of unique textures, (c) mesh decals for markings and wear, (d) instanced furniture. The repo's current approach (paint strips cut by a shader) already matches (c).
- **Kerb profile budget (inference, not sourced):** a kerb cross-section of 4 vertices (road-gutter edge, kerb face bottom, kerb face top with a 1-segment chamfer, sidewalk edge) gives 3 quads per sweep step. With smooth or weighted normals plus a normal-mapped arris, that reads as a bevelled stone. A 6–8 vertex profile with a real 2-segment bevel is the "hero/near" LOD. Far LODs can collapse the kerb into the sidewalk top surface, a flat strip with an albedo/normal line, because a 12–15 cm face covers under a pixel at city-builder distances. CS1's "10–100 tris typical LOD" supports dropping kerb geometry entirely at distance.
- Sweep steps on a curve should follow the curvature (fewer on straights), as the repo's greedy-insertion fitting already does. Kerb faces are thin, so a chordal error tolerance in metres can be looser than for the road's outer edge.

- I found no public documentation of CS2 kerb or sidewalk mesh construction, LODs or triangle counts. CS2 modding docs for networks were not found, and the [Paradox "adding custom assets" post](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/adding-custom-assets) does not cover roads.
- No source found for TF2 sidewalk or kerb geometry parameters (only file layout), SimCity 2013 road meshes, or GTA V or Watch Dogs kerb and sidewalk construction. There are no GDC talks on city kits in my results (Insomniac/Ubisoft not found).
- No source found on how any game models **dropped kerbs or curb ramps**. See section 5 for a sourced-by-analogy approach.

## 2. Trim sheets and texture arrays: fewer materials and draw calls; WebGL2 support (sampler2DArray, KTX2/Basis)

### Takeaway
Trim sheets (strips that tile on one axis) and texture arrays (one bound texture with N layers, chosen per vertex or instance) both let one material and shader cover kerb, gutter, paving and verge. That allows merging and instancing, so draw calls drop. three.js r186 supports `DataArrayTexture` and `CompressedArrayTexture` (WebGL2 `sampler2DArray`). `KTX2Loader` transcodes Basis Universal into the GPU's native compressed format, and its source imports `CompressedArrayTexture` for layered files.

### Cited Findings
- Trim sheets are textures that tile on the X or Y axis. Each row holds a different surface treatment (clean, worn, damaged). Fewer texture files means fewer shaders, which means fewer draw calls. Large environments may need several sheets, and contextual one-off details should be avoided. — [ArtStation, About the Trim Tool](https://www.artstation.com/a/34267016); [The Rookies entry](https://www.therookies.co/entries/7336); [Polycount discussion](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2780038/)
- A Polycount commenter says packing textures saves draw calls but you may run short of texture resolution, and that trims only need sheets when they are complex. — [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2780045/)
- Polycount points to the wiki "Modular environments" page and a trim sheet breakdown article as references. — [Polycount comment](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2648306); [Polycount trim-sheet tag](https://polycount.com/discussions/tagged/trim-sheet/p1)
- three.js `KTX2Loader`: it loads KTX 2.0 containers with Basis Universal data and transcodes "to a wide variety of GPU texture compression formats" chosen by `detectSupport(renderer)`, which must be called before loading. It needs WebAssembly, and the transcoder lives in `examples/jsm/libs/basis` (`setTranscoderPath`). — [three.js docs, KTX2Loader](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/KTX2Loader.html)
- The KTX2Loader source imports `CompressedArrayTexture` (alongside the cube and 3D variants), so multi-layer KTX2 files load as compressed array textures. — [unpkg, three@0.166.1 KTX2Loader.js](https://unpkg.com/browse/three@0.166.1/examples/jsm/loaders/KTX2Loader.js)
- `CompressedArrayTexture` is a 2D texture array from compressed data. `generateMipmaps` is always false, so mips must be in the file. `addLayerUpdate(i)` and `clearLayerUpdates()` upload single layers instead of the whole array. — [three.js docs, CompressedArrayTexture (mirror)](https://neofixer.arizona.edu/css/CSSOrbit/asteroidJS/three/docs/api/en/textures/CompressedArrayTexture.html); [three.js docs, DataArrayTexture (mirror)](https://neofixer.arizona.edu/css/CSSOrbit/asteroidJS/three/docs/api/en/textures/DataArrayTexture.html); example `webgl_texture2darray_layerupdate` in the three.js repo ([threejs.org examples](https://threejs.org/examples/#webgl_texture2darray_layerupdate))

### Inferences
- **Recommended layout:** a single `sampler2DArray` per map type (albedo+roughness, normal, plus optional AO/height) with layers like asphalt, asphalt-worn, concrete-slab sidewalk, brick/pavers, granite kerb, concrete kerb, gutter, grass verge, gravel, tactile paving. The layer index travels as a vertex attribute or instance attribute. All road and sidewalk meshes in a chunk can then be **one merged geometry with one material**, so one draw call per chunk per LOD.
- **Arrays vs trim sheets:** with arrays every layer can tile freely in both U and V with correct mip and filtering, and there is no atlas bleeding. A trim sheet tiles on only one axis, which suits kerbs (U along the kerb, V across the profile) but not 2D-tiling sidewalk paving. A hybrid is reasonable: a kerb *trim strip* as one array layer, where V maps across the kerb's top, arris and face.
- Array layers must share size and format, so pick one resolution (e.g. 1024² or 512²) for all layers and author the KTX2 arrays with `toktx`/`basisu`. Use UASTC for normal maps and ETC1S for albedo if memory dominates. This is general knowledge of Basis tooling, not verified in this session.
- In three.js the array sampler goes in through `onBeforeCompile` (MeshStandardMaterial) or a `ShaderMaterial`/TSL node. I did not verify the exact r186 TSL API for array textures in this session.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the official r186 `CompressedArrayTexture` page directly; a mirror of the docs was used. Behavior should be confirmed against r186 source.
- No benchmark numbers for draw call savings in three.js from arrays vs separate materials.

## 3. Stochastic texturing / hex tiling to hide repetition cheaply

### Takeaway
Mikkelsen's "Practical Real-Time Hex-Tiling" (JCGT 2022) drops Heitz & Neyret's histogram-preservation precomputation. It samples the **original texture 3 times with `textureGrad`** and blends with a weight exponent plus a contrast "gain". For normal maps it blends *surface derivatives* and supports per-tile random rotation. It is drop-in for WebGL2 (GLSL ES 3.0 has `textureGrad`, `dFdx/dFdy`) and is the best fit for large paving and asphalt areas. Cost is 3× the fetches of the base map, comparable to triplanar.

### Cited Findings
- Heitz & Neyret 2018: a triangle-lattice (simplex) grid where each vertex is the centre of a hex tile with a random offset, blending three hex tiles by barycentric weights. Plain linear blending loses contrast, so they use a histogram transform T and inverse T⁻¹ (precomputed textures that replace the source in the shader). — summarized in [Mikkelsen 2022, JCGT 11(3):5](https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/paper.pdf)
- Mikkelsen's adaptation omits histogram preservation, samples the original source texture, and adds a new blending metric plus a contrast function on the weights. He argues histogram preservation matters less for normal maps, which "form a single wide cluster". — [Mikkelsen 2022](https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/paper.pdf)
- Implementation details: weights are `W = Dw * pow(w, 7)`, normalized, with `Dw` lerped by a fall-off contrast of 0.6, then `Gain3(W, r)`. `r` is exposed, a conservative range is r ∈ [0.65, 0.75], and r = 0.75 was used for albedo and roughness. For normal maps the ramp is disabled (r = 0.5). Sampling must use `SampleGrad` because of the per-tile random offset. The paper says derivatives are blended because the derivative is linear, which makes this equivalent to blending height. — [Mikkelsen 2022](https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/paper.pdf)
- Random per-tile rotation works for normal maps only without histogram preservation; each sample is rotated into its tile's frame before blending. It removes the "same pebble, same orientation" repetition. — [Mikkelsen 2022](https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/paper.pdf)
- The paper's normal-map results are "strikingly similar" to histogram-preserving ones. For colour there is a minor trade-off that is visible mostly at hex borders. — [Mikkelsen 2022](https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/paper.pdf)
- Burley 2019 (JCGT 8(4):2) modified Heitz–Neyret to avoid lengthy preprocessing: per-channel LUTs from the histogram at load, a truncated Gaussian with soft clipping, optional luminance-only histogram preservation in YCbCr, and exponentiated weights against ghosting. — [Burley 2019, On Histogram-preserving Blending for Randomized Texture Tiling](https://www.jcgt.org/published/0008/04/02/paper.pdf)
- Unity's Procedural Stochastic Texturing (Deliot & Heitz 2019) needs "only three texture samples per fragment" plus a small LUT. A Unity developer says the cost is "basically similar to that of Triplanar Mapping". — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/procedural-stochastic-texturing-in-unity); [Unity forum prototype](https://discussions.unity.com/t/procedural-stochastic-texturing-prototype/732031)
- Inigo Quilez's alternatives: (1) random offset and rotation per tile, which leaves seams and mip line artifacts because of derivative jumps; (2) blending offset copies near borders; (3) a cheaper variant using a low-frequency noise index to pick and blend 2 of 8 offsets (2 fetches). — [iquilezles.org, texture repetition](https://iquilezles.org/articles/texturerepetition); GLSL port in [LYGIA sample/untile](https://git.gay/kaneraZh/lygia/src/tag/1.4.1/sample/untile.glsl); discussion in [jMonkeyEngine forum](https://hub.jmonkeyengine.org/t/techniques-to-reduce-terrain-texture-repeating-effect/45623?page=2)

### Inferences
- **Do not hex-tile structured, aligned patterns** (rectangular paving slabs, kerb stone joints, brick bonds). Random offset and rotation will break the joint grid. Use hex-tiling for *stochastic* content (asphalt aggregate, concrete noise, grass, gravel, grime and roughness detail). For paving, keep the base slab grid regular and break repetition with (a) a per-slab random tint and roughness from a hash of `floor(uv / slabSize)`, and (b) a hex-tiled stochastic *detail* map (grime, normal noise) layered on top. This is the cheap and high-quality combination.
- The cost can be capped: run hex-tiling only within a near distance, or only for the detail layer, and fall back to a single fetch beyond a distance (fade the weights to a single tile). This reduces fetch count at range, where repetition is less visible anyway. Quilez's 2-fetch variant is a cheaper middle LOD.
- WebGL2/GLSL ES 3.00 provides `textureGrad` and `dFdx/dFdy` natively, so the HLSL listings port 1:1 (`SampleGrad` → `textureGrad`, `ddx` → `dFdx`). The same works with `sampler2DArray` (`textureGrad(arr, vec3(uv, layer), dx, dy)`).

### Gaps
- No timings in the Mikkelsen paper text I read, and the Unity timings PDF was not opened. No WebGL-specific benchmarks were found.
- I could not open the Heitz & Neyret HPG 2018 paper itself; its description comes from Mikkelsen's and Burley's summaries.

## 4. Decals for wear, stains and cracks: deferred vs mesh decals in a forward renderer (three.js)

### Takeaway
three.js is forward-rendered, so G-buffer deferred decals need a custom pipeline. The cheap options are (a) **mesh decals**: thin geometry offset over the surface, merged or instanced and sharing one atlas or array material, and (b) **in-shader procedural wear masks** in the road and sidewalk material itself (zero extra draws). `DecalGeometry` is one draw call per decal and slow to generate on big meshes, so it is not suited to thousands of decals.

### Cited Findings
- three.js `DecalGeometry` chops a box-shaped chunk of the underlying geometry and builds a new mesh with projected UVs. It fits existing materials and forward rendering, but must be rebuilt when the source changes, is slow on large source meshes, breaks across multiple intersecting geometries, and costs a draw call per decal. Deferred and Forward+ decals have "very similar performance" to each other. — [three.js forum, How to draw stuff on stuff](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-draw-stuff-on-stuff/48934)
- Deferred decals can modify the G-buffer's base color, roughness and metalness, but deferred shading has a higher base cost than forward. — [Meta, Unreal deferred shading](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unreal/unreal-deferred-shading/)
- Unreal mesh decals: decals on separate geometry instead of projection. Large deferred decals draw front and back faces, so mesh decals need fewer draw calls and cover fewer pixels (flat back-facing decals cover 0 pixels), and they avoid stretching on curved surfaces. — [Unreal Engine 4.26 docs, Mesh Decals](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.26/WorkingWithContent/MeshDecals)
- City Sample uses custom meshes specifically for "road decals". — [Epic City Sample docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/city-sample-project-unreal-engine-demonstration)

### Inferences
- **Recommended for this project:** (1) bake wear into the base material with world-space masks, such as kerb-edge darkening from a distance-to-edge attribute, gutter grime from the profile V coordinate, oil stains in lane centres from the lane-UV channel, and cracks from a hashed array layer selected per slab. This costs no draw calls and no overdraw. (2) For discrete decals (manholes, patches, stains, drains), use instanced quads conformed to the flat road plane and sharing one array or atlas material. Use `InstancedMesh` with `polygonOffset` or a small vertical offset, and depthWrite false. That is one draw call for all decals of a chunk. (3) Avoid `DecalGeometry` at scale.
- Sidewalks and roads are mostly planar, so the projection-stretch problem that motivates mesh decals mostly applies only at kerb faces and ramps. Flat instanced quads are enough elsewhere.

### Gaps
- No three.js measurements of decal cost; Forward+ clustered decals in three.js are not documented in my sources.

## 5. Edge/bevel shading tricks to keep kerbs low-poly; dropped kerbs; verges blending with terrain; arc-length UVs along curves

### Takeaway
To keep kerbs at 3–4 quads per sweep step while looking bevelled, use a **one-segment chamfer with face-weighted (custom) vertex normals** so the flat top and face shade flat and only the chamfer shows a highlight, plus a normal-mapped rounded arris in the kerb trim strip. UVs along curves should be **arc-length parameterised**, not per point index, with separate UV channels for tiling base, along-road markings and masks. I found no sources on dropped kerbs or on verge-to-terrain blending in games; the suggestions below are engineering inferences.

### Cited Findings
- Face-weighted normals average adjacent face normals weighted by face area, so small bevel faces barely affect the big flat faces' shading. This is the common fix for low-poly hard-surface bevels. The trade-off is that bevels add vertices, and the method can break on organic or inset surfaces. — [Guild of Writers forum, area-weighted normals](https://forum.guildofwriters.org/viewtopic.php?p=23929); [Polycount, Eric Chadwick on face-weighted normals](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2796740); [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2796772)
- A bevel lets smoothing carry across the edge without splitting vertices, while a hard edge needs a split (UV and normal seam). Baked fake bevels on low-poly meshes need face-weighted normals and benefit from softer high-poly bevels and straight UVs to avoid wavy highlights. — [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2796772); [Polycount, baking bevels for low-poly isometric game](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2577181); [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2577165)
- Spline-road UVs should come from the distance along the spline, not point index: the total length divided by a repeat length sets the tiling. Uneven point spacing causes stretching. — [Unity forum, fabricating mesh for procedural path/spline](https://discussions.unity.com/t/fabricating-mesh-for-procedural-path-spline/746961); [Unreal Answers, tile textures on spline meshes](https://answers.unrealengine.com/questions/348972/view.html)
- BeamNG MeshRoad exposes `textureLength`, the metres per material repeat along the road. — [BeamNG docs, MeshRoad](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/levels/level_classes/meshroad/)
- Separate UV channels: planar for base textures (seamless, no stretch), continuous along-length for markings, normalized for masks. — [Fab road tool listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/34605f9d-e5e8-4a71-a64f-5bf230da274c)
- Junction UV seams are a known pain point. A SideFX user saw lines crossing junctions, fixed them in Blender with "Follow Active Quads", and found no procedural Houdini equivalent. — [SideFX forum, procedural UV unwrapping](https://www.sidefx.com/forum/topic/99355)

### Inferences
- **Panel correction on curves (inference):** on a curve, the inner kerb is shorter than the outer one. If U = arc length of the *centreline*, the inner edge compresses and the outer edge stretches. Compute U per profile vertex from *that vertex's own offset curve* arc length, i.e. U = s·(1 − κ·d) for signed offset d and curvature κ. Then snap the total to an integer number of kerb-stone lengths per segment (round(L/stoneLen)·stoneLen, with a scale ≤ ±5%), so stone joints never split at segment ends. For planar sidewalk paving, use **world-space XZ UVs** (optionally rotated to the street direction per segment, which is the "planar channel" above). That removes curve distortion entirely and makes junctions seamless.
- **Dropped kerbs / curb ramps (inference):** model them as a parameter of the same swept profile rather than separate meshes. Along an interval [s0, s1] at a crossing, interpolate the kerb-top height from full upstand (about 0.10–0.15 m) down to a flush lip (about 0 to 0.025 m), with transition flares on each side. The vertex count stays constant (same profile, different heights), so no new topology or draw calls are needed. Add the tactile-paving layer via the material's layer index or an instanced decal quad. Real-world dimensions should be checked against a standard such as UK DfT "Inclusive Mobility" or US ADA/PROWAG; I did not research them here.
- **Verges (inference):** make the verge a strip in the cross-section (between kerb back and the property line), textured with the grass array layer. For terrain blending, either (a) write the road and verge footprint into the terrain splat or mask texture so terrain shading switches to grass and dirt under and around the verge, with a soft falloff, or (b) give the verge strip outer vertices an alpha or weight that fades to the terrain material via the same array sampler and the terrain's world-space UVs. Option (b) only works if both share the same world-UV mapping, so the blended texels match. Option (a) avoids z-fighting and is cheaper at distance.
- **Low-poly kerb recipe:** profile = [gutter edge, face bottom, chamfer start, chamfer end, top back] = 5 vertices, 4 quads per step. Set custom normals: top vertices pointing up, face vertices horizontal, chamfer verts with averaged normals (face-weighted effect computed analytically, since the profile is known). Put the arris rounding and stone joints in the kerb trim strip's normal and albedo map, with U as the corrected arc length. At far LOD, drop the face and chamfer and fold the kerb into the sidewalk outline. This matches the CS1 typical LOD budgets.
